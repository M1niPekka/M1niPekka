# Notes to study sheet

Class notes are written fast and are usually a poor study tool: out of order,
half-abbreviated, missing the connective tissue. The job is to turn them into
something that supports recall.

## Handle the input

Notes arrive as pasted text, a file, a Drive doc, or a photo of a page or
whiteboard. Read the photo carefully — handwriting and diagrams both matter,
and a diagram often carries the point the words skipped.

**Flag gaps rather than filling them silently.** If the notes trail off, jump
topics, or contain something that looks wrong, say so explicitly: "your notes
stop mid-way through the second example" or "this looks like it should be
cell wall, not cell membrane — worth checking." Quietly patching holes from
general knowledge produces a sheet that disagrees with what the teacher taught,
and the teacher writes the test.

## Structure the sheet

- **Group by concept, not by the order things were said.** Lectures wander;
  study sheets shouldn't.
- **Lead each section with the idea in one sentence,** then the supporting
  detail underneath. This gives them something to recall toward.
- **Preserve the teacher's exact wording** for definitions, formulas, and
  anything flagged as important in the notes. Tests use the teacher's language.
- **Mark emphasis.** Anything the notes starred, boxed, underlined, or labeled
  "on the test" gets called out — that's the highest-signal content on the page.
- **Keep it to one page** where the material allows. If it genuinely doesn't
  fit, split by topic rather than shrinking to unreadable.

## Add what notes usually lack

- A short **vocabulary block** of terms used but never defined.
- **Worked examples** where the notes only have the formula.
- A handful of **self-test questions** at the bottom, so the sheet does double
  duty as a review.

## Deliver

Publish as an artifact for phone-friendly review, or produce a Word/PDF file if
they want to print it. Offer to turn the vocabulary block into flashcards
(`flashcards` skill).
