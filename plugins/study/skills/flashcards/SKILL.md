# Flashcards

Rereading notes feels like studying and mostly isn't. Retrieval — being asked
and having to produce the answer — is what moves material into memory. This
skill builds cards and then actually runs the quiz.

## Making the cards

Take the source (pasted notes, a file, a Drive doc, a photo of a handout) and
extract testable items. A good card has exactly one retrievable fact.

- **Split compound facts.** "The Treaty of Versailles was signed in 1919 and
  imposed reparations on Germany" is two cards, not one.
- **Ask forward and backward** for vocab and terms: term→definition and
  definition→term. Recognizing a word is not the same as producing it.
- **Prefer "why" and "when" over "what"** where the material supports it.
  "When do you use the quadratic formula instead of factoring?" tests judgment;
  "What is the quadratic formula?" tests only recall.
- **Cloze deletions** work well for definitions and processes — blank out the
  load-bearing word, not a throwaway one.
- **Cap it.** 20–30 cards per session. A 200-card deck the night before a test
  gets abandoned. If the source is huge, prioritize by what's most likely to be
  tested and say what you cut.

## Running the quiz

Default to quizzing interactively rather than dumping the deck as a list.

1. Ask one card. Stop. Wait for their answer.
2. Grade honestly — "close, but you're missing X" is more useful than "correct!"
   for a half-right answer. Do not inflate.
3. On a miss, give the answer plus a one-line hook (a mnemonic, a connection to
   something they got right) and put the card back in the rotation.
4. Track what's missed. Re-ask missed cards sooner and more often; retire cards
   they've hit twice in a row.
5. At the end, report what they know cold and what's still shaky — the shaky
   list is what they study next.

Keep the pace fast. One question per message, no preamble.

## Output formats

Offer the deck as a file when they want it outside the conversation:
- **CSV** (Front,Back) imports directly into Anki and Quizlet
- **A printable sheet** as an artifact for paper studying

Ask which they want rather than generating all three.
