# Study plan

Plan backwards from the deadline, not forwards from today. Forwards planning
produces schedules that run out of time; backwards planning produces schedules
that finish.

## Gather

- **The date, and what happens on it.** Test, essay due, project presentation.
- **The scope.** Chapters, units, topics. Get the study guide if one exists.
- **Real availability.** Not "how many hours could you study" but "what does
  your week actually look like" — practice, work, other classes' deadlines.
  Check Calendar if connected. Students consistently overestimate free time;
  build the plan for the pessimistic version and it survives contact with the
  week.

## Build

Work backwards from the deadline in this order:

1. **The day before is review only.** No new material. Reserve it.
2. **The final third is practice and self-testing** — past problems, practice
   tests, retrieval. Not rereading.
3. **The middle third is the hard topics** — whatever they're weakest at goes
   here, when there's still time to recover from discovering they don't get it.
4. **The first third is coverage** — get through the material once.

Then distribute:

- **Spaced, not massed.** Four 45-minute sessions across four days beat one
  three-hour block, by a wide margin. Say this if they propose the block.
- **Each session gets a specific, checkable goal.** "Study Chapter 7" is not a
  session. "Do problems 1–15 in 7.3 and list which ones I got wrong" is.
- **Build in one slip day.** Something will come up. A plan with no slack fails
  the first time life happens, and then gets abandoned entirely.
- **Revisit earlier topics briefly in later sessions.** Ten minutes of
  re-testing old material per session is what makes the early work stick.

## Deliver

A short table — day, session goal, roughly how long — is more useful than
prose. Offer to put the sessions on their calendar if Calendar is connected,
and to check in partway through.

Be realistic about what fits. If the scope genuinely doesn't fit the time left,
say so directly and help them triage what to drop rather than handing them a
plan you both know they can't run.
